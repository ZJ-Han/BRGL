# Balanced recyclable guide learning  

Sufficient labeled data are crucial for effectively training classifiers, but only a small fraction of them can be labeled due to the high cost of expert labeling. This mismatch between abundant unlabeled data samples and scarce labeled data samples remains a persistent challenge to training reliable classifiers for downstream tasks. To address this issue, we develop a Balanced Recyclable Guidance Learning (BRGL) framework that effectively leverages unlabeled data samples for classifier training. Specifically, the proposed BRGL trains two basic classifiers with the same architecture but different initializations, and alternates between mutual guidance and self guidance on the two classifiers. Mutual guidance alleviates the self-confidence bias that self guidance suffers from, while self guidance helps the two classifiers maintain the useful diversity on which mutual guidance depends. In addition, the proposed BRGL introduces a balanced data allocation scheme that allocates each unlabeled data sample to either mutual guidance or self guidance according to the classification diversity between the two classifiers, rather than relying on a fixed probability threshold. Furthermore, BRGL incorporates a recyclable strategy that dynamically updates pseudo-labels across training cycles, preventing incorrect pseudo-labels from accumulating during training. Extensive experiments on five public benchmark datasets show that the BRGL achieves competitive overall performance, especially when labeled data samples are scarce.
